A technical SEO audit that starts with what matters.

A practical order of operations for finding crawl, indexation, and performance issues before they hold back organic growth.

VP
Vrushang Patel
SEO Executive & Organic Consultant

Start with the pages that drive business

Before opening a crawler, identify your most valuable pages: product pages, service pages, comparison pages, and articles that support a buying decision. Group them by template. An issue affecting every product page deserves a different priority from an isolated broken link in an old post.

Separate crawling from indexing

A page can be crawlable without being indexed. Check robots.txt, response codes, canonical tags, and noindex directives separately. Submit only canonical, indexable URLs in your XML sitemap. Use Google Search Console’s URL Inspection tool on a small sample from each important template, and compare the selected canonical with the one you intended.

Trace internal paths

Start at the homepage and follow the links a visitor can actually use. Important pages should have relevant internal links from other useful pages. Check for orphan pages by comparing your crawl with sitemap URLs. Descriptive anchor text gives both visitors and search engines more context than repeated “learn more” links.

Measure real performance

Use field data where it is available to understand loading speed, responsiveness, and layout stability. Lab tools help diagnose causes, but a single score is not the whole story. Prioritize oversized images, unnecessary scripts, and unstable page elements on the templates that receive meaningful traffic.

Turn findings into an execution list

For each issue, record the affected URLs, business impact, proposed fix, owner, and verification step. Resolve blockers first, then work through template improvements. After release, recrawl the changed pages and monitor Search Console over time. An audit creates value when its recommendations become verified changes.
